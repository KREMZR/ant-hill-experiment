About the model

This is 1.4 by 0.9 metres of ground with one colony of black garden ants, Lasius niger. Everything is to scale: a worker is about 4 mm long and walks at roughly 18 mm a second.

No ant is told where the food is, and nothing in the program draws a trail. Each ant follows a few local rules taken from published studies, and the trails, the rush to a new food source and the way round a rock all come out of that.

What each ant does

  • Smells with two antennae. It compares the trail pheromone at its left and right antenna and turns toward the stronger side. The turn depends on the relative difference, not the absolute one.
  • Keeps count of its own steps and turns. This running estimate of where the nest is, called path integration, is how it gets home. It is accurate to a few percent, so far from home the estimate drifts. Select an ant to see it.
  • Marks a trail only when the trip paid off. On the way home with food it dabs pheromone from the tip of its abdomen. Better food makes marking more likely. It marks less on a trail that is already strong.
  • Remembers one food site. A forager that found food goes straight back to it, and trusts that memory over the trail. After two empty visits it gives up on the site.
  • Recruits nestmates. A forager that marked the trail stirs others into leaving. At the entrance each recruit picks a direction with odds that rise steeply with the pheromone there.
  • Walks on an alternating tripod. Front and hind leg on one side move with the middle leg on the other. Zoomed in, each foot stays planted while the body passes over it.
  • Stops to check other ants. Ants that meet head-on often pause and touch antennae before passing.

What is simplified

  • The inside of the nest is not drawn. Food storage, the queen, brood and resting workers are tracked as numbers.
  • The colony's life cycle runs 300 times faster than the ants' behaviour, so you can watch it grow. One colony day lasts about five minutes at 1× speed.
  • An ant's brain is not simulated. Its decisions are rules whose outcomes match what has been measured, with strengths tuned where no measurement exists.
  • There is no weather, no day and night, no predators and no rival colonies.

Saving

The colony is saved in this browser every few seconds. Close the page and it will be here when you come back, having lived through the time you were away, up to six hours of it. A colony you paused stays exactly as you left it. The save is not shared with other browsers or devices, and starting a new colony replaces it.

Studies the rules are based on

  • Beckers, Deneubourg and Goss (1992, 1993). Trail laying, U-turns and trail lifetime in Lasius niger.
  • Deneubourg, Aron, Goss and Pasteels (1990). The choice function for picking between trails.
  • Perna and others (2012). Individual rules for trail following; the response follows Weber's law.
  • Grüter, Czaczkes and Ratnieks (2011). Foragers trust their own memory over the trail.
  • Czaczkes, Grüter and Ratnieks (2013). Ants mark less on busy, well-marked trails.
  • Mailleux, Deneubourg and Detrain (2000). Scouts mark only after getting a full load.
  • Müller and Wehner (1988); Wittlinger, Wehner and Wolf (2006). Path integration and step counting.
  • Zollikofer (1994). Stepping patterns of walking ants.
  • Bossert and Wilson (1963). How far and how long an alarm signal carries.
